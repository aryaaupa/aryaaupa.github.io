About me

I like problems that refuse to stay inside one discipline.

I grew up between India, the United Kingdom, and the United States. Moving across different systems made me pay attention to the invisible infrastructure around people: healthcare, education, public policy, and the technologies that quietly determine who receives help and who falls through the cracks.

My first serious research work focused on biodegradable nanorobots and environmental sensing. I became fascinated by the idea that an intelligent device could do something useful in the physical world and then disappear without leaving another permanent object behind.

Later, through research and clinical experiences, I kept seeing a different version of the same systems problem. Patients are often given a good treatment plan, but the plan breaks down between appointments. Physical therapy exercises become repetitive. Feedback disappears. Clinicians have limited visibility. The patient is expected to carry the whole system alone.

The future of medicine is not only better diagnosis. It is better support between diagnosis and recovery.

Today, I work at the intersection of medicine, computation, and sensing. I care about technologies that can understand human movement, detect meaningful biological or environmental signals, and respond in ways that are useful, safe, and actually usable.
