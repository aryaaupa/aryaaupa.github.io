RESEARCH VISION

01
// THE PROBLEM

Modern nanotechnology has an exit problem. Sensors and embedded devices deployed in biological and environmental systems accumulate indefinitely — creating the very contamination they were designed to study. Conventional approaches treat material permanence as a given. I treat it as a design flaw.

02
// MY APPROACH

I engineer transient nanosystems — devices with programmed degradation pathways that perform their function and disappear. This demands fluency across polymer chemistry, nanoelectronics, edge computing, and ML-based predictive modeling. My work at Columbia and CUNY operationalizes this across theory and wet-lab experiment.

03
// IMMEDIATE FOCUS

My current work targets soil microplastic detection using battery-less biodegradable nanorobots (Patent IND 202421082687) and the characterization of polymer degradation kinetics via FTIR and Raman spectroscopy. Both streams feed into a unified model of sensing-to-decomposition lifetime optimization.

04
// PHD TRAJECTORY

I am pursuing doctoral research to formalize the theoretical foundations of transient nanoelectronics — developing generalized stability models for energy-constrained nanoscale embedded systems and extending biodegradable intelligence toward in-vivo biomedical applications, where material impermanence is not just ecological but essential.
