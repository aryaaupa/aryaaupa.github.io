Stuff

Tools, systems, and small opinions.

I spend an unreasonable amount of time optimizing how I research, build, travel, and stay organized. This page is for other stuff-enjoyers. Nothing here is sponsored.

Research stack

Zotero
The least painful way I have found to organize papers and citations.
ResearchRabbit
Useful when I know one strong paper and need to understand the research neighborhood around it.
Overleaf
Where most serious writing eventually goes, usually after living in a chaotic document first.
MATLAB
Still one of my defaults for signal processing, modeling, and experimental data.
ArcGIS Pro
What I use when a research question becomes spatial.
Notion
Lab notes, project planning, startup documents, and probably too many databases.

Lab things

Carbon cloth electrodes
Flexible, conductive, inexpensive, and useful for early electrochemical prototypes.
Portable potentiostats
A real quality-of-life improvement when every measurement should not require a full bench setup.
Glass sample jars
Somehow one of the most consistently important pieces of research equipment.
Label maker
Not scientifically interesting. Scientifically essential.

Medical and health technology

Apple Watch
One of the most accessible platforms for thinking about continuous health data and patient behavior.
WHOOP
I like seeing recovery as a longitudinal signal instead of a feeling from one morning.
Complete Anatomy
Useful for visualizing anatomy while thinking about movement, rehabilitation, and patient education.
MedBridge and Physitrack
Helpful references for understanding how home exercise programs are delivered and where the experience still breaks.

Currently testing

On-device exercise tracking
How much useful movement feedback can happen without sending patient video to the cloud?
Voice interfaces for rehab
Exploring whether spoken coaching can make home exercise feel less isolating.
AI coding agents
Sometimes transformative, sometimes an extremely confident intern who misunderstood the assignment.

Things I think are overrated

Productivity systems that require more maintenance than the work
Healthcare websites that say “revolutionizing” without explaining anything
Research posters with full paragraphs in 11-point font
AI products that add a chatbot to an unchanged workflow
Collecting health data without giving the patient a useful next action
Meetings that could have been a clearly written document

Small things that help

Writing tomorrow’s first task before going to sleep
Recording equipment model numbers before an advisor asks
Keeping experimental decisions in the notebook, not only final numbers
Taking photos before dismantling physical setups
Sending the follow-up while the conversation is still fresh
Carrying a snack on days that accidentally become twelve hours long
